6. Discovery Questions (Use These on Calls)

Use SPIN-style probing — Situation → Problem → Implication → Need-payoff. Don't pitch until you understand their pain.

Situation Questions

  • How many racks do you currently operate? Mostly air-cooled or any liquid cooling?
  • What's your current PUE? (If they don't know — that's data point #1, they probably need monitoring)
  • What sensors do you already have deployed? Vendor?
  • Do you have a BMS or DCIM platform? Which one?
  • How are alerts currently routed today — email only? On-call?

Problem Questions

  • When was the last time you had a thermal incident that required physical intervention? How was it handled?
  • What percentage of your op-ex goes to cooling? Are you tracking that monthly?
  • Are you running any AI/GPU workloads or planning to? How is your cooling handling the density?
  • How do you decide when to bring more cooling capacity online?
  • What does it cost you per hour when a hot aisle goes above safe temps?

Implication Questions (the painful ones)

  • If a critical incident happened at 2am on a Saturday, how long until someone is responding?
  • What's your current cooling efficiency vs your design target? What does the gap cost annually?
  • If your PUE improved from X to Y, what's the dollar value? (Help them calculate)
  • The 2026 IRS guidance pushed Section 179D up to $5.36/sq ft (was $1.88) for energy-efficient retrofits, retroactive to 2023 placed-in-service dates — has your team filed at the new rates yet?
  • If your facility could generate $200K-$500K per year from currently-wasted heat, what would that change?

Need-Payoff Questions

  • If we could give you AI that automatically adjusts cooling before an incident becomes a problem, what would that be worth?
  • If you could see in real-time the dollar impact of every cooling decision, would your team make different choices?
  • What would it mean for your team if AI handled the routine cooling tuning so they could focus on bigger problems?

7. Common Objections + How to Handle Them

"We already have monitoring through our BMS — we don't need another tool."
"Most BMS systems show you data. We use AI to act on it. Quick question — when your CRAC is running at 78% fan and a hot aisle spikes, does your BMS automatically increase the fan speed and quantify the dollar impact, or does it just alert someone? That's the gap we fill. We integrate with your existing BMS via webhook — we don't replace it, we make it 10x smarter. Want to see what that looks like in 5 minutes?"
"We don't trust AI to control our equipment."
"That's exactly why we built it with permission-first defaults. Out of the box, AI proposes actions and waits for your approval — nothing happens automatically until you set explicit permission rules. Most clients start with full manual approval, then over time they auto-approve low-risk actions like 'enable economizer when outside air drops below 55°F.' Plus every action is logged with full audit trail — for compliance and to protect you if anything goes sideways."
"Splunk or Vigilent already does this."
"Splunk is generic infrastructure monitoring — they don't understand cooling specifically and they don't take cooling actions. Vigilent is the closest comparison, but they're $50K-$200K+/year and require enterprise sales cycles. We give you the same AI auto-action capability at $599/month with self-serve onboarding. The difference: we're built ground-up for AI/GPU density. Theirs was built before AI workloads existed."

8. Section 179D — The "Money Left On The Table" Lever

The deduction was expanded by the Inflation Reduction Act, not expiring. The pitch is "most operators haven't captured it at the new rates" — not artificial deadline pressure.

Section 179D federal tax deduction — IRA-expanded, not expiring.
  • $5.36 per square foot for energy-efficient retrofits in 2026 (was $1.88 pre-IRA)
  • Requires prevailing wage + apprenticeship compliance for the top rate; base rate is lower without it
  • Retroactive to systems placed in service since 2023
  • For a 100,000 sq ft data center: typically $500K–$2M in deductions on cooling, lighting, or envelope work already done
  • For a 50,000 sq ft facility at full rate: $268K deduction
  • Most operators either haven't filed at all or filed at the old $1.88/sq ft rate — money on the table.

Talking points:

  • "You're not missing a deadline — you may have already missed the rate change."
  • "Every cooling improvement done since 2023 may qualify retroactively at the new rate."
  • "The 179D specialty firms (KBKG, alliantgroup) charge 20–30% of the deduction value to file. We help you scope what qualifies, then either file directly or refer to a partner CPA — we don't charge contingency."
  • Reference: "Most CFOs I talk to are still operating on the pre-IRA numbers. The ones who've updated are pulling cooling projects forward to maximize the deduction."

Honesty note for solo-founder positioning: 179D filings require a licensed P.E. to stamp the energy analysis (in most states). ThermaShift can identify qualifying improvements and scope the deduction; the actual certification should be referred to a partner firm (KBKG, alliantgroup, or Walker Reid Strategies). Don't oversell direct filing capability on calls.

14. Red Flags — When to Disqualify

Not every prospect is a good fit. Walk away from:

  • Facilities under 50kW total IT load — economics don't work for them
  • Prospects who only want "free advice" with no buying intent across multiple touchpoints
  • Companies with active litigation around cooling — wait for legal resolution
  • Prospects asking for white-label as a pre-qualification at the Watch tier — that's an Enterprise conversation, requalify
  • Facilities locked into multi-year vendor contracts with no exit path until 2028+
